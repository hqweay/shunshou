Use case · Instagram image download

Every original image in an Instagram post, in one click

Saving one image today means opening the carousel, right-clicking, saving, renaming — and doing it ten times for ten images. Shunshou compresses that into one step: open the post, click Download Instagram images, and the whole carousel comes back at once. Preview each one, copy it, or hit Download all (N). Images are fetched in your own browser — no third-party download site, and no pasting the post link to anyone.

Why it's a chore

Saving one image runs into six walls

The task itself is trivial. What makes it annoying is that every route leaves something behind: the wrong size, a pile of repeated clicks, or handing the link to someone else.

⇲

Right-click saves a thumbnail

Save image as usually gets you a compressed, smaller version that turns to mush when enlarged. The full-size URL was already loaded by the page — it just never reaches your hands.

▤

Ten images, ten rounds

Open one, right-click, save, rename — then do it again. Miss one and you get to hunt for it.

⛔

Download sites want your link

Paste the post URL into a site you don't know: ads, countdown timers, and sometimes your images are uploaded to their server first before you get them back.

◫

You want the original, not a re-layout

A share image rearranges the post into a card. Here you want the image itself — unchanged, ready to use as material, reference or archive.

⌘

Caption, author and date come along

Save the pictures and the source is gone: who posted it, when, and what the caption said. Finding the post again means searching from memory.

␡

Good luck finding them later

Files land as hash strings, mixed into your downloads folder, with no way to tell which post they belong to.

How it works

Downloading Instagram images takes three steps

No connection to configure, no account, no extra Instagram login. The action follows the post page — it only shows up on /p/… and /reel/… URLs.

1. Open the post page

Any post or Reels page on instagram.com. Whatever login state your browser is already in is what gets used — you're not asked to sign in.

2. Click "Download Instagram images"

The action first pulls the post's complete image list, so a carousel gives you more than the one image you happen to be looking at.

3. Pick in the dialog

Preview one by one: Copy this image pastes straight into a chat or document, Download this image saves one, Download all (N) saves the set. Nothing is written to disk by default.

What the action does

Originals, the whole set, previewed before saving

In one sentence: it turns "the images in this post" into a set you can preview, copy and download — and the decisions stay yours.

⇉

An Instagram carousel, fetched in one pass

Carousel images are completed automatically (the page itself only mounts the current few; the action fetches the post's full list). Up to 20 images per post.

◆

Originals, not thumbnails

It uses the large image URL the page itself already loaded — no re-compression, no watermark, no resizing.

⛨

No third party in the middle

Fetching happens in your own browser: there is no server of ours, images are not uploaded or relayed, and no download site gets your link.

👁

Look first, then save

Images stay in the result dialog. Nothing is written to disk unless you click download, and nothing is filed away behind your back.

⧉

Copying is often faster

Most of the time you just want it in a chat, a doc or a note — "Copy this image" skips the whole save-then-find-the-file round trip.

🏷

Filenames you can read

The default name is username-date, not a hash — so a folder of downloads still tells you which post each image came from.

The other exit from the same post

If what you want is a good-looking card, that exists too

Downloading originals and making a share image are two different exits, both built on the same extracted data: one hands you the files, the other rearranges the post into a card — author, images, caption and date in place, with a QR code in the corner that leads back to the post.

FAQ

Questions about downloading Instagram images

Are my images uploaded to your server?

No. Shunshou has no server: images are fetched in your browser (the extension requests them from its local background script; the userscript uses its script manager's request capability). Nothing is uploaded, nothing is relayed.

The only outbound traffic is to instagram.com, to fetch this post's page and its images.

Do I need to log in to Instagram?

No extra login. The action reads the post's public data (the post page and the official embed page), and uses whatever state your browser is already in.

It does not bypass login walls and does not touch private accounts: if it can't get the data it says so plainly, rather than guessing or impersonating you.

Can it download videos or Reels?

For now it can only fetch a Reel's cover image; the video itself is not part of this step. That boundary is written here on purpose, so you don't install it for the wrong reason.

How many images at once, and what if some are missing?

Up to 20 images per post; duplicate links are removed. Any single image over about 7 MB is skipped, and the result tells you how many failed.

Failures are counted separately rather than reported as success — open the run details to see which step it stopped at.

Where do the downloaded files go?

Nothing is saved by default: images stay in the result dialog until you click Download all (N), which writes them to your browser's own download folder (default filename: username-date).

If you'd rather not touch the disk at all, "Copy this image" gets the image just as well.

Does it work on mobile?

The extension is desktop-only; on mobile you'd install the userscript. Instagram keeps pushing you into its app on mobile browsers, though, so this action is more comfortable on a computer.

Does it cost anything? Do I need an account?

It's free, with no ads and no paid features, and no account to register. Your setup lives only in your own browser.
